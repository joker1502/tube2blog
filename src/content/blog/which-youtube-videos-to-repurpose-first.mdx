Not every YouTube video you've published deserves to become a blog post. Repurposing the wrong ones wastes hours and delivers zero SEO returns.

The problem isn't that repurposing doesn't work — it's that repurposing indiscriminately doesn't work. If you have a back catalog of 50, 100, or even 200 videos, starting at video #1 and working chronologically is the slowest path to results.

The better approach: score each video against four criteria, repurpose the highest-scoring ones first, and watch your blog traffic grow from day one instead of month six.

## The Hidden Cost of Repurposing Every Video

The standard advice sounds simple — repurpose everything. Publish a video on YouTube, turn it into a blog post, share it on social media, embed it in an email. Rinse and repeat and the traffic will follow.

That advice sounds productive but hides a dangerous assumption: that every video is equally worth repurposing.

### Time Is Your Scarce Resource

A single YouTube-to-blog conversion takes seconds with [Tube2Blog](https://tube2blog.com). But the full repurposing workflow — editing the draft, formatting headings, adding internal links, sourcing images, writing meta descriptions, scheduling, and promoting — takes 30-60 minutes per post even with automation.

If you have 100 videos in your back catalog, that's 50 to 100 hours of work. Not every video will earn that investment back in search traffic, especially when you're just starting out.

### SEO Results Follow a Power Law

In content marketing, roughly 80% of your organic traffic comes from 20% of your content. The same distribution applies to repurposed video content. A handful of your videos — the ones that answer high-intent search queries, target evergreen topics, and already have strong engagement signals — will drive the vast majority of your blog traffic.

The rest will sit in your sitemap, indexed but unvisited.

The goal isn't to repurpose everything. It's to find the 20% of videos that will generate 80% of the results, then repurpose those first.

### The Comparison Trap

Comparing yourself to creators who repurpose every single video is tempting but misleading. Those creators either have dedicated teams, produce content in a narrow niche where every video targets search intent, or they've been at it long enough that even their low-performers accumulate residual traffic over years.

For a solo creator or small team starting a [repurposing workflow](/blog/repurpose-youtube-to-blog-posts), prioritization isn't optional — it's the difference between seeing results in weeks versus feeling burnt out in months.

## Why Some Videos Perform as Blog Posts and Others Don't

Not all YouTube content translates equally well to written format. Three core factors determine whether a video will drive organic traffic as a blog post.

### Search Intent Alignment

The most critical question: Are people actually searching for this information?

Tutorial videos — how to do X, how to fix Y, how to choose Z — align perfectly with search intent. Someone types "how to edit videos in Premiere Pro" into Google, and a blog post repurposed from your Premiere Pro tutorial can appear in the results.

Vlogs, personal updates, and entertainment content typically lack search intent. Nobody types "my trip to Japan vlog day 3" into Google. Repurposing these into blog posts rarely generates organic traffic, no matter how well they perform on YouTube.

### Evergreen vs. Timely Content

Evergreen content — tutorials, guides, reviews, comparisons, and definitions — stays relevant for years. A blog post about "how to create a content calendar" will still attract visitors in 2028 because the core question doesn't change.

Timely content — algorithm updates, event recaps, seasonal topics — has a short shelf life. A post about "2026 YouTube algorithm predictions" peaks within weeks and fades to zero traffic within months.

When prioritizing your back catalog, lean toward evergreen videos. They compound their SEO value over time, while timely content is a sprint, not a marathon.

### Content Depth and Structure

Videos that cover a topic comprehensively — step-by-step tutorials, detailed explanations, multi-faceted comparisons — provide enough material for a substantive blog post. Every point in the video can become a section, heading, or paragraph.

Short opinion videos or quick tips rarely have enough substance to stand alone as a blog post. They work better as part of a larger roundup or listicle rather than as individual articles.

## The 4-Factor Scoring Framework

Here's a practical system for rating every video in your catalog. Score each factor from 0 to 10, then add them up. Videos scoring 28 or higher are your top repurposing candidates.

### Factor 1: Search Potential (0-10)

How many people search for this topic each month?

| Score | Signal |
|-------|--------|
| 8-10 | High-volume keyword with clear search intent |
| 5-7 | Medium-volume or moderately competitive topic |
| 3-4 | Low search volume but specific enough to rank quickly |
| 0-2 | No meaningful search volume |

Check YouTube search suggestions for your video title — if autocomplete suggests multiple related phrases, search potential is high. Cross-reference with a keyword tool when possible.

### Factor 2: Engagement Signal (0-10)

What does your YouTube analytics say?

| Score | Signal |
|-------|--------|
| 8-10 | Top 10% of your catalog by views or watch time |
| 5-7 | Above-average performance for your channel |
| 3-4 | Average or slightly below |
| 0-2 | Lowest performing videos |

A video that already resonates on YouTube signals that the topic has an audience. That same audience exists on Google too.

### Factor 3: Evergreen Value (0-10)

How long will this content remain useful?

| Score | Signal |
|-------|--------|
| 8-10 | Relevant for 3+ years |
| 5-7 | Relevant for 12-24 months |
| 3-4 | Relevant for 6-12 months |
| 0-2 | Relevant for less than 6 months or purely news-based |

### Factor 4: Content Density (0-10)

How much blog-worthy material does the video contain?

| Score | Signal |
|-------|--------|
| 8-10 | 10+ minutes with clear sections and examples |
| 5-7 | 5-10 minutes with structured content |
| 3-4 | 3-5 minutes or loosely structured |
| 0-2 | Under 3 minutes with minimal substance |

### Putting It Together

For each video, calculate: **Search Potential + Engagement + Evergreen + Density = Total Score**

| Score Range | Action |
|-------------|--------|
| 32-40 | Repurpose immediately — these are your goldmine videos |
| 28-31 | Repurpose this week — strong candidates |
| 20-27 | Repurpose when you have spare capacity |
| Below 20 | Skip — focus on higher-scoring videos first |

This framework takes roughly 30 minutes to evaluate your top 60 videos. That's a one-time investment that saves 20-plus hours of repurposing low-ROI content.

## Gut Feeling vs. Data-Driven Selection

Choosing videos by instinct sounds faster but costs more in the long run:

| Method | Time per Decision | Accuracy | Typical ROI |
|--------|------------------|----------|-------------|
| Gut feeling | 30 seconds | Low — biased toward recent or favorite videos | Repurpose 40, get 3 performers |
| 4-factor scoring | 5 min per batch of 10 | High — objective across all videos | Repurpose 10, get 8 performers |
| Repurpose everything | No decision needed | Zero — wastes time on low-potential content | Repurpose 100, get 5 performers |

The scoring approach is the clear winner for anyone serious about using [video content for SEO growth](/blog/repurpose-youtube-videos-for-seo-guide).
