You've been repurposing YouTube videos into blog posts for a few months. Traffic is coming in. But when someone asks whether the time investment is worth it, you don't have a clear answer. You know it's working — the analytics dashboard shows green lines going up — but you can't connect the dots between a specific video you repurposed and the organic traffic it generated.

This is a measurement gap, and it's more dangerous than not measuring at all. Without proper attribution, you can't tell which repurposing strategies are working, which topics to double down on, and whether you're spending time on the right videos.

Measuring the SEO impact of repurposed content isn't complicated. It requires three things: the right metrics, proper tracking setup, and a consistent review cadence. Here's exactly how to build that system.

## The Metrics That Actually Matter for Repurposed Content

Not all metrics are created equal. Vanity metrics like page views and social shares feel good but don't tell you whether your repurposing strategy is driving sustainable growth. Focus on the metrics that connect directly to search performance and business outcomes.

### Organic Impressions and Clicks

These are your foundational metrics. Google Search Console reports impressions (how many times your repurposed post appeared in search results) and clicks (how many times someone clicked through). Track both for every repurposed post.

The ratio between them, click-through rate (CTR), tells you whether your title and meta description match search intent. A post with high impressions but low CTR (under 2%) needs a title or description rewrite. A post with high CTR but low impressions needs better internal linking and topical coverage to signal relevance to Google.

For repurposed content specifically, compare the organic clicks on your blog post against the estimated search traffic your video was getting before repurposing. This comparison — which most creators never make — tells you whether the blog post is reaching a different audience than the video.

### Average Position Trend

A single post's ranking position matters less than the trend over time. A post that moves from position 25 to position 12 over three months is on the right track, even if it hasn't hit page one yet. The [SEO guide for video content](/blog/seo-for-video-content-guide) covers how Google evaluates content freshness, and repurposed posts benefit from the same pattern — they tend to gain ranking momentum over 2-4 months as Google recognizes their relevance.

Track position at the cluster level, not just individual posts. If you repurposed five videos about the same topic, look at the average position of all five posts together. A cluster lift (where all posts move up together) is a stronger signal of topical authority than a single post jumping in rankings.

### Indexing Rate

This is the most overlooked metric for repurposed content. How quickly does Google index your repurposed blog posts compared to your original content?

Repurposed posts from YouTube transcripts have a structural advantage: they're comprehensive, well-structured, and typically contain natural keyword density. If your repurposed posts are indexing slower than your original posts, check for technical issues — missing sitemap entries, slow crawl budget, or thin content flagged by Google's systems.

Use the URL Inspection tool in Google Search Console to check indexing status for each repurposed post. If a post isn't indexed after two weeks, investigate why.

### Engagement Signals on Page

Time on page, scroll depth, and bounce rate matter more for repurposed content than for original content. Here's why: a visitor who lands on a repurposed blog post from search has a different intent than someone watching the original video. They want a quick answer, not a 15-minute explanation. If your repurposed post keeps them reading for 3+ minutes and they scroll through 70%+ of the content, the format is working. If they bounce in under 30 seconds, the post isn't matching the search intent.

Compare these engagement metrics against your site's average for original blog posts. If repurposed content consistently underperforms, the issue is usually one of two things: the post is too long for the search query (you kept everything from the video), or the structure doesn't let readers scan and find answers quickly.

## How to Set Up Tracking Before You Publish

Most creators set up tracking after publishing — if they set it up at all. This is backwards. Proper tracking starts before you repurpose your first video, because you need a baseline to measure against.

### Step 1: Document Pre-Repurposing Video Performance

Before you repurpose a YouTube video, record its current metrics: views, average view duration, key search terms driving traffic, and the video's ranking position for its primary keyword. Screenshot the YouTube Analytics dashboard for each video you plan to repurpose. You'll compare these against the blog post's performance in 30, 60, and 90 days.

This baseline is critical. Without it, you can't answer the most important question: did the blog post reach people the video wasn't reaching?

### Step 2: Tag Your Repurposed Posts

Create a consistent naming or tagging convention for all repurposed content. In Google Search Console, you can filter by URL pattern — so use a consistent slug structure. Or add a custom dimension in Google Analytics 4 that tags each post as "repurposed-from-video."

A simple approach: include the video ID in the blog post's metadata or in a custom field. This lets you connect every repurposed post back to its source video when you run reports.

### Step 3: Set Up Keyword Tracking

Choose 2-3 primary keywords per repurposed post and track their positions before and after publication. Free tools like Google Search Console's performance report work well for this. Export the keyword data monthly and look for movement.

Pay special attention to keywords where the original YouTube video was ranking. If your video ranked #8 for "how to edit video in Premiere Pro" and your repurposed blog post starts moving from #25 toward #10 over 90 days, you're capturing search traffic that the video alone wasn't getting — users who prefer reading over watching.

### Step 4: Create a Performance Dashboard

Build a simple spreadsheet or use Google Looker Studio to track each repurposed post's performance over time. Columns should include:

| Field | Source | Update Frequency |
|:------|:-------|:----------------|
| Post title and URL | CMS | On publish |
| Source video title and URL | YouTube | On publish |
| Primary keyword | Keyword research | On publish |
| Pre-repurpose video position | YouTube Analytics | On publish |
| Week 1 impressions and clicks | GSC | Weekly |
| Month 1 keyword position | GSC | Monthly |
| Month 3 keyword position | GSC | Monthly |
| Month 6 keyword position | GSC | Monthly |
| Cumulative organic clicks | GSC | Monthly |

This dashboard is your single source of truth. After 3-4 months, you'll have enough data to identify patterns: which types of videos produce the best blog performance, which keywords transfer well from video to text, and which repurposing strategies deliver the highest ROI.

## How to Read the Data: From Raw Numbers to Strategic Decisions

Having data is useless without knowing what to do with it. Here's how to interpret the numbers and adjust your strategy.

### The 30-Day Check: Indexing and Initial Signals

In the first 30 days, don't worry about rankings. Focus on indexing and early engagement. If your post is indexed within 1-2 weeks and generates at least some impressions, the technical foundation is solid. If it's not indexed after 14 days, request indexing via GSC and check for content quality issues.

Look for early CTR signals. If impressions are growing but CTR is below 1%, your title and description need work. If the post has zero impressions after 30 days, the topic might not have search demand — or your internal linking isn't strong enough to tell Google the post exists.

### The 90-Day Check: Ranking Trends

At 90 days, most repurposed posts should show a clear ranking trajectory. Posts moving consistently upward are keepers — invest more in internal links and consider updating them with fresh information. Posts stuck at position 20+ with no upward movement need a strategic decision: invest in improving them (better structure, more internal links, richer content) or cut losses and focus on higher-potential topics.

Compare the post's trajectory against the original video's trajectory. If the blog post outperforms the video for the same keyword within 90 days, you've confirmed that written content fills a gap your video audience wasn't covering.

### The 6-Month Check: ROI Calculation

At six months, calculate the real ROI. Total organic clicks from the post, estimated traffic value (using a conservative CPC model), and the time invested in repurposing and editing. Divide traffic value by time invested to get your hourly ROI.

For most repurposed content, the ROI improves over time because the post continues accumulating traffic with zero ongoing effort. A post that took 45 minutes to repurpose and generates 200 monthly organic visits at 12 months is earning roughly 4-5 visits per minute of creation time — a ratio that's hard to beat with any other content format.

### When to Cut a Repurposed Post

Not every repurposing attempt succeeds. Cut your losses on posts that show zero indexing after 30 days, zero organic clicks after 90 days, or a consistent downward ranking trend with no intervention points. You can always revisit them later with a different angle.

[Prioritizing which videos to repurpose first](/blog/which-youtube-videos-to-repurpose-first) reduces your failure rate significantly — but some experiments will fail regardless. The goal isn't 100% success. It's identifying the 20% of repurposed posts that drive 80% of the results.

## Repurposed Content vs. Original Content: A Realistic Performance Comparison

| Factor | Original Blog Post | Repurposed Video Post |
|:-------|:-------------------|:---------------------|
| Creation time | 2-4 hours | 30-60 minutes |
| Time to first ranking signal | 2-4 weeks | 1-3 weeks (transcript content is naturally detailed) |
| 90-day average position | 8-15 | 10-18 |
| 6-month average position | 5-12 | 7-15 |
| Content depth | Variable — depends on research | Naturally thorough — transcript preserves detail |
| Keyword targeting | Intentional — designed for SEO | Organic — emerges from video content |
| Video audience crossover | None | Captures readers who prefer text over video |
| Sustained effort | Requires fresh research per post | Source video provides the raw material |

The data reveals a clear trade-off. Repurposed content takes less time to produce and indexes faster, but original content tends to rank slightly higher on average because it's designed specifically for search intent from the start. The winning strategy combines both: use repurposed content to build volume and topical authority quickly, then layer in original content for high-value, competitive keywords.

## The Measurement System That Pays for Itself

Setting up proper tracking takes about two hours. Maintaining the dashboard takes 15 minutes per month. In return, you get clear answers to the questions that matter: which repurposing strategies work, which topics to prioritize, and whether your time investment is paying off.

The alternative — guessing — is what most creators do. They publish repurposed content, see some traffic, assume it's working, and never dig deeper. Six months later, they can't explain why some posts perform and others don't.

Start with the tracking setup before your next repurposing session. Document one video's current performance. Tag the blog post when you publish it. Check GSC after 30 days. Build the habit of measurement, and your repurposing strategy will evolve from hopeful experimentation to data-driven execution.
